The whole accounting cycle

From supplier bill to signed financial statements

Modules are role-gated — each person only sees what they're allowed to, and the books stay balanced underneath it all.

📘

General Ledger

A proper chart of accounts, manual and recurring journals with reversals, GL inquiry that drills to the voucher, and the Books of Accounts — general journal, ledger, cash receipts and disbursements — printable for BIR registration.

🧾

Accounts Payable

Purchase orders, goods receipts and supplier bills matched three ways. Withholding tax computed on every bill, payment batches gated by a delegation of authority, post-dated checks tracked to maturity, and 2307 certificates produced per payee.

💰

Accounts Receivable

Sales invoices issued only from BIR-registered series. Credit limits gate the delivery, collections apply OPEN-ITEM to the oldest invoices, aging snapshots and expected credit losses keep the receivable honest, and dunning letters go out on schedule.

🏦

Treasury

Bank statements import and auto-match to the books; the rest flows into a reconciliation worksheet a second person reviews. Petty cash runs on a strict imprest float, deposit slips cover same-day collections, loans carry amortization schedules.

👥

Payroll

Compensation masters flow into semi-monthly runs. Philippine runs compute SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and withholding tax per the current tables, with 13th month accrual, loans, statutory remittances and 2316 certificates. Gulf runs withhold nothing and accrue end-of-service gratuity by tenure instead.

🧮

Tax, per country

In the Philippines: 2550M/Q VAT returns with sales & purchase books behind them, 1601C and 1601EQ expanded withholding with alphalists, quarterly RCIT vs MCIT, NOLCO tracking. In the Gulf: VAT returns at the local rate (or none, in Qatar and Kuwait), corporate tax or Zakat on the country calendar. One filing calendar drives them all.

📦

Inventory & Fixed Assets

Goods receipts relieve received-not-invoiced, weighted-average costing with a full movement ledger, counts with variance posting and NRV checks. The fixed-asset register feeds certified depreciation batches straight into the general ledger.

🗓️

Period Close

A close checklist per period — accrual runs, reconciliations, gate checks — signed off item by item, then soft-close and hard-close with reason codes. Financial statements generate from the closed books, with comparative columns.

🛡️

Controls & Audit

Segregation-of-duties rules and an approval matrix decide who may touch what. Every field change is stamped with the user, the time and the reason. Scheduled checks raise exceptions into a queue that must be resolved, not ignored.

Philippine Tax

Returns that reconcile to your own books

  • VAT transactions are captured as sales and purchase books post — 2550M/Q builds from them, not from re-typing
  • Expanded withholding on every bill and payment; 1601C/1601EQ plus the alphalists fall out of the same data
  • Quarterly income tax compares RCIT against MCIT with NOLCO application, before you provision
  • The filing calendar watches due dates and queues what's unfiled — correspondence and protests tracked too
Disbursements

Nobody pays a bill alone

  • Supplier bills approve against budget and commit funds; the 3-way match closes the loop with the PO
  • Payment batches release only past the delegation of authority — preparer and releaser must differ
  • Checks print from inventory with numbers that reconcile; post-dated checks mature onto a schedule
  • Every released peso lands on a bank statement line that the reconciliation must explain
Period Close

A close you can hand to the auditor

  • The checklist for each period is generated for you — accruals, bank recs, subledger ties, tax returns
  • Gate checks refuse a hard close while subledgers still disagree with the control accounts
  • Financial statements generate from the closed books — statement of financial position, income statement, cash flows
  • Every sign-off carries who, when and what evidence — the audit file builds itself as you close

Questions

Good to know

Does it work outside the Philippines?

Yes — the system is multi-jurisdiction. At setup you pick the country: Philippines, United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain or Kuwait. The pack that follows changes the currency, the VAT rate and return forms (or no VAT at all in Qatar and Kuwait), the filing calendar, the payroll rules — Gulf companies accrue end-of-service gratuity instead of SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG and withhold nothing — and which screens exist at all. A group can run a Philippine entity and a Gulf entity side by side in the same install, each with its own pack.

Is this real double-entry accounting or a billing front-end?

Real. Every transaction — a supplier bill, a collection, a payslip, a depreciation batch — posts balanced journal lines to the general ledger, through the same books a CPA would keep: sales journal, cash receipts, cash disbursements, purchases, general. The trial balance is the same data your financial statements generate from.

Does it produce the BIR forms?

The VAT returns (2550M/Q), expanded withholding returns (1601C, 1601EQ, 1604E) and compensation (1604C) are built from your own transactions, with the supporting schedules and alphalists. Sales invoices are issued only from registered series tied to your ATP / permit-to-use, so numbering gaps and voids are tracked the way the regulations require.

Can we import our existing books?

Yes. A guided migration loads your chart of accounts, opening balances, partner masters and open items — unpaid invoices on both sides, post-dated checks, employee advances — and validates that the opening entry balances before anything is saved.

Can several people use it at the same time?

Yes. Staff PCs connect to the Host over your office network — the desktop app or a browser. One server process owns the single database and runs every query in order, so there is never a write conflict. A team of dozens is well within range.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything runs on your own network, fully offline — accounting, payroll, reports, backups. Once installed, the app never phones home.

Where is my data stored?

In one SQLite database file on the Host machine's disk, plus an uploads folder for receipts and documents. It is never sent anywhere. Backups run on a schedule you set, and a restore drill inside the app verifies the backup is actually readable.

How does it stop one person from doing everything?

Segregation-of-duties rules and an approval matrix are enforced by the server, not the screen: the person who prepares a payment batch can't be the one who releases it, claims without receipts can't leave draft, and every decision lands in an approval inbox with a reason. Each field change is stamped with who, when and why.

Can we brand it with our company?

Yes. Upload your logo, set your company details and pick theme colours — it flows through the app and the printed reports.
