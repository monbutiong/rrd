Pharmacy & POS

Batch-tracked stock, sold at the counter

  • Medicines carry batches and expiry dates; costing is weighted average, dispatch is FEFO
  • The POS terminal sells medicines, supplies and equipment as walk-in sales
  • Payments by cash, card or insurance; receipts print, and voids restore every unit back to stock
  • Controlled, refrigerated and low-stock items surface with their own filters
Procurement & requisitions

Buy once, receive once, post straight to stock

  • One purchase order engine handles medicine, supply and equipment lines — currency and tax aware
  • Receiving (GRV) dispatches stock by item type: medicines to pharmacy, supplies to inventory, equipment to the asset register
  • Departments raise supply requisitions; approval, issue and rejection follow one workflow
  • FEFO issue skips expired batches and falls back to the flat balance when a line isn't batch-tracked

Questions

Good to know

Is it a real hospital system, or a spreadsheet with a UI?

It runs a full life cycle per patient — registration, admission, clinical encounters, lab and imaging orders, ward placement, billing, and pharmacy dispensing — plus procurement, inventory, POS sales, canteen, employees, scheduling and reporting. Every field change is stamped with the user, the time and the reason.

How do pharmacy and stock work?

Medicines and supplies are batch-tracked where needed — each item carries its expiry date, costing is weighted average, and dispensing and issue follow FEFO (earliest expiry first, expired batches excluded). A GRV on a purchase order posts received stock straight into the right place by item type, and the POS terminal sells it as a walk-in sale.

Can it handle billing and partial payments?

Yes. Each visit accumulates itemized charges into an invoice; you can record cash, card or insurance payments in part, track the running balance, and void with a full audit trail. Collections roll up into the daily revenue report.

Can several people use it at the same time?

Yes. Staff PCs connect to the Host over your hospital network — the desktop app or a browser. One server process owns the single database and runs every query in order, so there is never a write conflict. A hospital of dozens of users is well within range.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything runs on your own network. The only time the internet matters is confirming the Microsoft Store licence occasionally; after that it runs offline for months at a time.

Where is my data stored?

In one SQLite database file on the Host machine's disk, plus an uploads folder for photos and documents. It is never sent anywhere. You can back it up, restore it, or move it to another PC from inside the app.

How is it licensed?

It's a paid Microsoft Store app with a 30-day free trial. The licence is tied to your Microsoft account; a copied install won't run on another machine. One purchase covers one server — staff PCs connect to it and need nothing of their own.

Can we brand it with our hospital?

Yes. Upload your logo, set your hospital details and pick theme colours — it flows through the app and the printed reports.
